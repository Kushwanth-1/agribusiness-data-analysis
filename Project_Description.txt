This project builds an early-warning yield forecast for a fictional Nagpur agritech start-up. The goal is to predict district-level crop yield at the end of week 20 of the agricultural year (about mid-October), early enough to guide procurement, and to explain the drivers to business users. No dataset came with the brief, so I generated a seeded synthetic dataset of about 350,000 weekly rows across 6 districts and 6 crops that reproduces the problems described: prices mixed between per quintal and per kg, missing monsoon rainfall, yield recorded in wrong units, plus duplicates and messy labels. I cleaned each issue with rules detected from the data, audited columns for leakage (post-harvest production, procurement and price columns), and used a time-aware split: train 2006 to 2019, validation 2020 to 2022, test 2023 to 2025. I engineered 50 features, including lag, rolling and ratio features, with past-only target encoding for high-cardinality categories. I compared a baseline, ridge regression, random forest and gradient boosting using expanding-window cross-validation, then tuned the best one. The final model beats the 3-year-average baseline by 42% on validation and 31% on test (test error 1.19 q/ha per district-crop), and Shapley values and partial dependence show rainfall and price signals as the main drivers. I converted forecasts into procurement actions (book early, wait, routine) and checked them against the 2025 actuals. I also report what did not work: test error was 40% above validation largely in unusually wet seasons, which doubled in frequency, so the stability goal was missed; a simple recalibration made forecasts worse; and I explain one wrong prediction in detail. All results come from simulated data, so they demonstrate the method rather than real yields.
